PROFILE SCORING METHODOLOGY

Purpose

The scores are comparative research aids for the current transcript archive.
They are not objective measurements of a person's real-world worth, power,
dangerousness, guilt, intent, or moral character.

COMICSGATE / COMMUNITY POWER LEVEL — 0 TO 10,000

The power score combines:

- appearances across the complete transcript archive;
- total configured name and alias mentions;
- number of distinct sources or channels;
- number of episodes attributable to the identity's own source;
- prominence in episode titles;
- recency within the archive.

Bands:

- 9,000–10,000: Central / system-shaping
- 7,000–8,999: Prominent
- 5,000–6,999: Established
- 3,000–4,999: Recurring
- 1,000–2,999: Peripheral but identifiable
- 0–999: Trace / insufficient corpus evidence

“THREAT TO COMMI3 MARK” — PUBLIC CONFLICT / EXPOSURE RISK — 0 TO 10,000

This user-requested score is restricted to public-content risk. It combines:

- appearances and mentions in Commi3 Mark streams;
- persistence across separate months;
- prominence in episode titles;
- conflict-oriented vocabulary in matching transcript contexts;
- reciprocal references to Commi3 Mark in attributable own-source episodes;
- a small reach component from the community power score.

It does not assess physical threat, criminal propensity, private intent,
mental state, or moral character.

Core Russian Troll Factory panelists receive a collaboration adjustment so
ordinary panel mentions do not falsely appear as conflict. Commi3 Mark's
self-risk score is zero.

Bands:

- 8,000–10,000: Very high sustained public-conflict exposure
- 6,000–7,999: High public-conflict exposure
- 4,000–5,999: Moderate public-conflict exposure
- 2,000–3,999: Low public-conflict exposure
- 0–1,999: Minimal or insufficient evidence

OBSERVABLE COMMUNICATION STYLE

Style sections are generated only when the archive contains enough material
attributable to a matching source or channel. They measure caption vocabulary
related to:

- evidence and sources;
- uncertainty and qualification;
- assertive truth or falsehood claims;
- adversarial or controversy language;
- humor and laughter;
- questions.

These are lexical observations about public captions, not personality
diagnoses. Played clips, guests, automatic-caption errors, and missing speaker
labels can contaminate the measurements.

PRIVACY

Profiles use public identities only. They must not reveal, connect, or
speculate about separate legal identities or sensitive offline attributes.
