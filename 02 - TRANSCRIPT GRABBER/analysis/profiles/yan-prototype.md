## Executive assessment

Stencil Artist Yan is a public creative identity using **@YansOnly**, a core recurring panelist on Commi3 Mark's *Russian Troll Factory* (RTF), and a publicly identified contributor to Indie Comics UK. Public profiles describe Yan as a stencil artist, voice actor, host of *Yansplaining*, and slam poet. The clearest independent biography says Yan returned to the creative industry after a long absence to help build a UK comic-book resource. The available record supports a hybrid role: visual art, voice/performance work, creative-community infrastructure, and long-form ensemble commentary.

Yan's RTF function is more specific than the earlier automated baseline suggested. Episode-level evidence confirms live participation in at least seven reviewed 2026 installments and additional appearances extending back to 2024 and earlier. The February, May, June, and July transcripts repeatedly cue Yan's closing "found poem," a format that turns memorable fragments from the broadcast into an improvised recap. The public self-description “slam poet” independently supports treating this as a recurring creative role rather than a one-off gag.

The strongest supported characterization is:

> A UK stencil artist, voice performer, indie-comics resource contributor, and recurring ensemble commentator whose distinctive RTF role combines panel discussion with found-poem/slam-poetry recaps.

The independently indexed footprint is real but incompletely archived. It spans X, YouTube, Instagram, Dailymotion, Indie Comics UK, and historical Comicsgate.org listings, but no legal identity, private biography, complete channel archive, or complete art portfolio is established here.

The matcher should therefore use five result classes:

1. confirmed Yan identity form — retain timestamp and context;
2. RTF-context probable Yan form — retain for lightweight review;
3. other identified person or identity — group separately;
4. ordinary word/currency noise — aggregate counts only;
5. unresolved short-name hit — preserve context and never promote automatically.

Attendance must be inferred separately from mention matching. Intro music, discussion of absence, chat messages, references to earlier poems, and played clips can all produce true identity mentions without proving live participation.

## Full-corpus mention rescan

A 22 July 2026 rescan searched 158 unique raw transcript JSON records. Duplicate files and derived intelligence reports were excluded. The scan tested exact and loose forms including `Yan`, `Jan`, `Yen`, `Ian`, `yawn`, `Stencil Artist`, `StencilArtistYan`, and `YansOnly`.

Results:

- 172 exact `Yan`/`Yan's` caption segments across 27 videos;
- 26 of those videos are RTF-attributed records;
- 11 `Stencil Artist` renderings across ten RTF-attributed videos, including “Stencil Artist Yan,” “stencil artist Jan,” and truncated forms;
- one exact `Yan` hit in a non-RTF-attributed record, rejected after context review because it refers to Shane Davis and Yanzi/Yonzi of Nine Lives;
- 17 `Yen` hits across eight videos, mostly Japanese-currency references, with only a small RTF-context subset plausibly rendering Yan;
- 33 `Ian` hits across 18 videos, dominated by other named people and therefore unsuitable as a global alias;
- five `yawn` hits, all ordinary-word noise;
- no genuine caption occurrence of the intact handle `YansOnly`.

The 26-video RTF footprint is a mention footprint, not an attendance ledger. It includes intro music, chat references, discussions of Yan's absence, previous-poem callbacks, and live participation. It strengthens the conclusion that Yan is a durable roster identity while preserving the manually reviewed attendance table as the stronger source for presence.

No genuine external-source transcript mention of Yan is established in the current local corpus. The verified YouTube, X, Instagram, Dailymotion, Comicsgate.org, and Indie Comics UK records establish an independent public footprint, but the transcript grabber has not yet backfilled Yan's own channel. A channel backfill is still required before a responsible standalone communication-style assessment can be made.

The key methodological result is negative as well as positive: expanding a three-letter identity indiscriminately creates large amounts of unrelated material. The production scanner should prefer full-name and RTF-context forms, count obvious noise, and send short-name ambiguity to review.

The public record supports a person who helps sustain two collective projects: a UK indie-comics resource and a recurring commentary panel.

The best future profile development is evidence attachment, not speculation. New material should expand the attendance ledger, identify attributable creative work, and preserve the difference between public role, self-description, and independently verified credit.
